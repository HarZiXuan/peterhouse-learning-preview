# Peterhouse Learning Review: how to use it

This guide shows how to use the Learning Review, step by step, with examples. You do not need any technical knowledge.

---

## What the system does, in one minute

Twice a year, in **June** and **December**, every child gets a Learning Review: a short, colourful report for their family, with photos and a few warm paragraphs about each learning area.

The system makes this quick:

1. **The teacher answers simple questions** about the child, such as "Mia counts objects to 10", by choosing how often they see it.
2. **The system writes the report** in the school's style, from those answers.
3. **The teacher reads and adjusts it**, then sends it to the administrator.
4. **The administrator approves it**, and the family's PDF is ready to download.

In December, the report also shows each child's June ratings beside the new ones, so families can see how their child has grown.

---

## Who uses it

| Role | Who, usually | In short |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Administrator** | Principal or school manager | Sets up the school, controls staff accounts, approves every report. |
| **Lead teacher** | The main teacher of a class | Writes the whole report for each child in their class and sends it for approval. |
| **Co-teacher / subject teacher** | A second class teacher, or e.g. the Chinese teacher | Writes only the learning areas given to them, and passes them to the lead teacher. |

Lead, co- and subject teachers all have a **Teacher** account. What they can do depends on how the administrator sets up each class. A teacher only ever sees the classes they teach.

**Our example school, used throughout this guide**

- **Mrs Wong** is the principal (Administrator).
- **Ms Lim** is lead teacher of **K1 Sunflower**.
- **Mr Tan** teaches Chinese (华文) in K1 Sunflower.
- **Mia** is a child in K1 Sunflower.

---

## The rating scale

For each statement, the teacher picks one of these. Families see the words and stars, never a number.

| Rating | What it means | Example: "Mia counts objects to 10" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ★☆☆☆☆ **Still learning** | Just starting, needs a lot of help | Mia counts to 3 with a teacher pointing. |
| ★★☆☆☆ **Sometimes** | Can do it now and then, with help | Mia sometimes counts to 10 but often skips numbers. |
| ★★★☆☆ **Regularly** | Does it most of the time | Mia usually counts 10 blocks correctly. |
| ★★★★☆ **Confidently** | Does it easily, on her own | Mia counts any 10 objects without help. |
| ★★★★★ **Really shines** | Does it on her own and helps or inspires others | Mia counts and helps her friends count too. |
| **Not seen yet** | The teacher has not had the chance to observe it | Never written as a weakness. |

## For teachers

### Logging in for the first time

1. Mrs Wong creates your account and gives you a **temporary password**.
2. Go to the school's Learning Review website and log in with your email and that password.
3. You will be asked to **choose your own password** (at least 10 characters).

> **Forgot your password?** Ask the administrator to reset it. After five wrong tries your account locks for 15 minutes; the administrator can unlock it straight away.

### What you see

- **My classes:** your class(es), each child, and how far each report has got. You also see what's on today and this week.
- **Children's reports:** every report you need to write, with a number showing how many are still to do.
- **My calendar:** your class's timetable, school events, closures and report deadlines. You can add it to your Google or Outlook calendar.
- **Settings:** your account and password.

### Lead teacher: writing a child's report

*Example: Ms Lim writes Mia's December report.*

1. **Open Mia** from **Children's reports**.
2. **Go through each learning area** (English, Chinese, Mathematics, Social-Emotional Development, Self-Enrichment). For each statement, pick a rating.
   - *"Mia names simple shapes"* → **Regularly**.
3. **Add a short note** if you have one. Keep it to what you saw.
   - *"Loved the shape hunt in the garden, found 6 circles."*
4. **Answer "What did Mia love most?"**
   - *"Painting with sponges."*
5. **Add photos** (up to three per area). They only print if Mia's parents have given photo consent.
6. When every statement is rated, press **Write report**. In a few seconds the whole report appears.
7. **Read it carefully.** Anything the system is unsure about is **highlighted in gold**, for example a number, a word like "weak", or another child's name. Fix or remove it.
8. **Change anything you like:**
   - Click a paragraph and type to edit it, or
   - Ask for one paragraph again, e.g. **Make it shorter**, or type what you want: *"mention her sponge painting"*.
9. When you are happy, press **Submit for review**. It goes to Mrs Wong.

> **Changed a rating after writing?** The paragraph for that area is marked as out of date. Read it again and save it, or write that part again.

> **Want it back?** While it is waiting for review, press **Take back** to change something, then submit again.

### Co-teacher or subject teacher: writing your part

*Example: Mr Tan writes the Chinese part of Mia's report.*

1. Open Mia. You only see the **Chinese (华文)** area, because that is yours. The questions are in Chinese.
2. **Rate each statement** and add a note, just like above.
3. Press **Write my page**. The Chinese paragraph is written, in Chinese.
4. Read it and change it if needed. You can type what to change and press **Write it with this**.
5. Press **Approve and send to Ms Lim**.

Ms Lim's report now uses **your paragraph exactly as you sent it**. If you need to change it later, open it and take it back, then send it again.

> **Ms Lim cannot write the whole report until every teacher has sent their part.** The system tells her who it is still waiting for, e.g. *"Still waiting for Mr Tan (Chinese)."*

### When a report comes back

If Mrs Wong presses **Request changes**, you will see:

- a **"Changes requested"** mark on the report in your list, and
- her note at the top of the report, e.g. *"Please add something about Mia's drawing."*

Make the change and press **Submit again**.

### Downloading the family's PDF

Once Mrs Wong approves a report, **Download PDF** appears on it. Before approval the button explains why it is not ready yet.

## For the administrator

*Example: Mrs Wong, the principal.*

### What you see

- **Classes:** every class in the school, grouped by age level (Playgroup, Nursery 1, Nursery 2, Kindergarten 1, Kindergarten 2).
- **Children:** every child in the school.
- **Reports:** every report and where it stands, plus the questions teachers answer.
- **Calendar:** the school calendar and each class's timetable.
- **Teachers:** every teacher, their classes, and how many reports they have written.
- **Settings:** school details, subjects, staff accounts, the activity record, and the report period.

### Controlling staff accounts

Go to **Settings → People and sign-ins**.

| You want to… | Do this | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add a new teacher | **Add person**, enter name and email, choose **Teacher**. Give them the temporary password shown. | A new teacher, Ms Devi, joins in July. |
| Change someone's details | **Edit** their name, email or role. A new email becomes their login. | Ms Lim's email changes after marriage. |
| Help someone who forgot their password | **Reset password**, and give them the new temporary one. | Mr Tan can't remember his password. |
| Unlock a locked account | **Unlock**. They can try again at once. | Ms Lim typed the wrong password five times. |
| Stop someone logging in | **Deactivate** their account. They are logged out everywhere immediately. | A teacher leaves the school. |

> Making someone an **Administrator** gives them access to the whole school, so only do this for school leaders.

### Setting up a class

*Example: setting up K1 Sunflower.*

1. **Classes → New class.** Name it "K1 Sunflower" and choose the level **Kindergarten 1**.
2. **Choose the teachers:** Ms Lim as **lead teacher**, others as co-teachers.
3. **Choose who writes each area:** set **Mr Tan** for Chinese. Areas you leave alone are written by the lead teacher.
4. **Add children:** enter each child's details and **birthday**. The system works out the right level from the birth year and only offers classes at that level.
   - *Mia turns 5 this year, so she can only go into a K1 class.*
5. **Photo consent:** tick it on the child's profile if the parents have agreed. Without it, no photo can be added for that child.

You can move a child to another class at the same level, or mark a child as having left the school.

### Approving reports

1. Open **Reports**. The number beside it shows how many are **waiting for review**.
2. Open Mia's report. You see it **exactly as the family will**, plus Ms Lim's ratings and notes, and what changed since the last version.
3. Choose one:
   - **Approve:** the report is final and the PDF can be downloaded.
   - **Request changes:** write a note, e.g. *"Please add something about Mia's drawing."* It goes back to Ms Lim.
   - **Take over:** finish it yourself, for example if Ms Lim is away. Ms Lim can read it but not change it while you have it.

Approved reports are **frozen**: if anything changes later (a teacher leaves, a photo is replaced), the family's report stays exactly as approved. If you spot a mistake after approving, you can **Send back** the report.

### Editing the questions

**Reports → Questions** lists the statements teachers rate, for each learning area and age level.

- **Reword** a statement: it stays the same skill, so June and December still compare.
  - *"Mia counts to 10"* → *"Mia counts objects to 10"*
- **Replace** a statement with a new skill: the old one is retired, and the new one starts fresh (no June comparison).
- When you are done, press **Publish**. Reports already started keep the questions they began with.

### Calendar and timetable

- **Calendar → Add an entry:** an event (e.g. *"Library visit, Friday 10:00"*), a school closure, or a report deadline. Choose who it is for: the whole school, some levels, or some classes.
- **Timetable:** set each class's weekly lessons, e.g. *Mon 9:00 English, 9:45 Chinese*. Teachers see it on their calendar.
- **Settings → Subjects:** the subjects on the timetable and their colours.

Teachers can see the calendar but cannot change it.

### Starting a new semester

Go to **Settings → Report period**.

- **June to December:** every child gets a new, blank December report in the same class with the same teachers. June's reports stay exactly as they were, and their ratings appear beside December's.
- **December to the next June (new school year):** every class moves up a level with its children and teachers, and Kindergarten 2 children are marked as graduated.

Reports not yet approved when you move on are **not lost**. They can still be finished and approved afterwards. If you moved on too soon, you can go back.

### The activity record

**Settings → Activity log** shows who logged in (and failed to), who opened a child's record, and who wrote, changed, approved or downloaded a report.

*Example: a parent asks who has looked at their child's report. Mrs Wong checks the activity log and sees exactly who opened it and when.*

## Common questions

**Does the AI know who my child is?**
No. Before anything is sent to the writing assistant, the child's name becomes "[Child]", other children become "[Friend]" and teachers become "[Teacher]". Phone numbers and emails are removed. The name is put back on the school's own server afterwards.

**Can the AI make things up?**
It is told to write only from the teacher's ratings and notes, and never to invent events, places or people. Anything doubtful is highlighted for the teacher, and nothing reaches a family without the teacher's check and the administrator's approval.

**What if the internet or the AI is down?**
The system writes the report from the school's own sentence bank instead, and tells you. Your ratings are always safe.

**Can a teacher see another class's children?**
No. A teacher only sees the classes they teach. A child from another class simply does not appear.

**Will a family see a low score as a number?**
No. Families see stars and words only. "Not seen yet" is never written as a weakness.

**What does the December report show from June?**
Each statement shows June's rating beside December's, with a **green ↑** where the child has improved and a **red ↓** where they have slipped back.
